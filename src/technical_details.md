# Drishti: Technical Theory & Mathematical Foundations

This document provides a rigorous, first-principles derivation of the physical, mathematical, and algorithmic principles governing the Drishti Assistive Wearable.

---

## 1. Optical Geometry & Spatial Raycasting

### 1.1 The Pinhole Camera Model
Light rays passing from a 3D physical world point $\mathbf{P}_w = [X_w, Y_w, Z_w]^T$ through the camera's optical center onto an image sensor plane are modeled using projective geometry.

In homogenous coordinates, the transformation from world coordinates to camera coordinates is given by the extrinsic matrix:

$$\mathbf{P}_c = \begin{bmatrix} X_c \\ Y_c \\ Z_c \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{R} \mathbf{P}_w + \mathbf{t}$$

where $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ is an orthogonal rotation matrix ($\mathbf{R}^T \mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}$, $\det(\mathbf{R}) = 1$) and $\mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the translation vector.

The projection of $\mathbf{P}_c$ onto the 2D discrete pixel coordinate plane $\mathbf{p} = [u, v, 1]^T$ is governed by the camera intrinsic matrix $\mathbf{K}$:

$$\lambda \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{K} \mathbf{P}_c = \begin{bmatrix} f_x & 0 & c_x \\ 0 & f_y & c_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} X_c \\ Y_c \\ Z_c \end{bmatrix}$$

where:
- $f_x, f_y$ are the focal lengths expressed in pixel units along the horizontal and vertical sensor axes: $f_x = \frac{F}{\mu_x}$, $f_y = \frac{F}{\mu_y}$ (where $F$ is physical focal length in mm and $\mu$ is physical pixel pitch).
- $c_x, c_y$ represent the principal point (the intersection of the optical axis with the sensor plane).
- $\lambda = Z_c$ represents the metric depth along the optical z-axis.

### 1.2 Lens Distortion Compensation
Real-world compact wearable lenses (e.g., Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 wide-angle lenses) exhibit radial and tangential barrel distortion. The undistorted normalized camera coordinates $(x_n, y_n) = (X_c / Z_c, Y_c / Z_c)$ are related to the distorted coordinates $(x_d, y_d)$ via Brown-Conrady polynomial modeling:

$$r^2 = x_n^2 + y_n^2$$

$$x_d = x_n (1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6) + 2 p_1 x_n y_n + p_2 (r^2 + 2 x_n^2)$$

$$y_d = y_n (1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6) + p_1 (r^2 + 2 y_n^2) + 2 p_2 x_n y_n$$

In Drishti, all pixel bounding box centroids $(u_b, v_b)$ generated by the detector are first undistorted using inverted calibration polynomials before spatial raycasting.

### 1.3 Back-Projection to 3D Rays
Given an undistorted bounding box centroid $(u, v)$, the unit ray vector $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ pointing from the optical center into 3D space is:

$$\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \frac{\mathbf{K}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}}{\left\| \mathbf{K}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\|_2}$$

The horizontal azimuth angle $\theta_{az}$ and vertical elevation angle $\phi_{el}$ relative to the forward optical heading are:

$$\theta_{az} = \arctan\left(\frac{u - c_x}{f_x}\right), \quad \phi_{el} = \arctan\left(\frac{v - c_y}{f_y}\right)$$

## 2. Time-of-Flight (ToF) Physical Principles & Sensor Fusion

### 2.1 Photon Transit Physics
The VL53L1X distance sensor operates via direct Time-of-Flight (dToF). A 940 nm Vertical-Cavity Surface-Emitting Laser (VCSEL) emits periodic optical pulse trains. The reflected photons strike an array of Single-Photon Avalanche Diodes (SPADs).

The distance $d$ to the nearest reflective surface is directly proportional to the round-trip transit time $\Delta t$:

$$d = \frac{c \cdot \Delta t}{2}$$

where $c \approx 2.9979 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$ is the speed of light in air. 

Statistical photon arrival time is captured via Time-to-Digital Converters (TDCs). The signal-to-noise ratio is affected by ambient photon arrival (Poisson noise):

$$\text{SNR} = \frac{N_{signal}}{\sqrt{N_{signal} + N_{ambient}}}$$

### 2.2 Conical Field of View & Multi-Zone RoI
The VL53L1X emits light in a conical cone of acceptance with a full Field-of-View (FoV) of approximately $27^\circ$. The sensor's $16 \times 16$ SPAD array allows programming custom Regions of Interest (RoIs) down to $4 \times 4$ SPADs.

Let $\mathbf{C}_{ToF}$ represent the optical axis of the ToF sensor, and let $\theta_{FoV} = 27^\circ / 2 = 13.5^\circ$ be the half-angle. A 3D point $\mathbf{P}_c$ lies within the ToF cone if:

$$\frac{\mathbf{P}_c \cdot \mathbf{C}_{ToF}}{\|\mathbf{P}_c\| \|\mathbf{C}_{ToF}\|} \ge \cos(\theta_{FoV})$$

### 2.3 Ray-Cone Metric Fusion
Let a detected object have bounding box $[u_{min}, v_{min}, u_{max}, v_{max}]$. Its angular extent span is $[\theta_{min}, \theta_{max}]$ horizontally and $[\phi_{min}, \phi_{max}]$ vertically.

If the angular footprint of the bounding box overlaps with the ToF conical acceptance window:

$$\mathcal{S}_{overlap} = \left[\theta_{min}, \theta_{max}\right] \cap \left[-\theta_{FoV}, +\theta_{FoV}\right] \neq \emptyset$$

Then the scalar metric distance $d_{ToF}$ reported by the sensor is projected along the centroid ray $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$:

$$\mathbf{P}_{object} = d_{ToF} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{r}} = \begin{bmatrix} X_{obj} \\ Y_{obj} \\ Z_{obj} \end{bmatrix} = d_{ToF} \begin{bmatrix} \sin\theta_{az} \cos\phi_{el} \\ \sin\phi_{el} \\ \cos\theta_{az} \cos\phi_{el} \end{bmatrix}$$

When an object lies outside the active ToF cone, depth is estimated using bounding box prior geometry and camera height above ground $H_{cam}$:

$$Z_{est} \approx \frac{f_y \cdot H_{real}}{h_{pixels}}$$

where $H_{real}$ is the statistical mean height of the classified class (e.g., $1.7\text{ m}$ for person, $0.8\text{ m}$ for chair).

## 3. Deep Learning Edge Quantization & Roofline Analysis

### 3.1 Affine Quantization Formulation (FP32 to INT8)
Edge inference engines (e.g., Raspberry Pi 5 CPU NEON SIMD, Hailo-8L NPU) achieve high throughput and energy efficiency by replacing 32-bit floating-point arithmetic with 8-bit integer operations.

Continuous real values $x \in [\alpha, \beta]$ are quantized into integer values $q \in [q_{min}, q_{max}]$ (where for signed INT8, $q_{min} = -128$ and $q_{max} = 127$) using the affine mapping:

$$x \approx S \cdot (q - Z)$$

$$q = \text{clamp}\left(\left\lfloor \frac{x}{S} \right\rceil + Z, \; q_{min}, \; q_{max}\right)$$

where:
- $S \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the arbitrary positive real scale factor:
  $$S = \frac{\beta - \alpha}{q_{max} - q_{min}}$$
- $Z \in \mathbb{Z}$ is the integer zero-point ensuring exact representation of real zero (crucial for zero-padding in convolutions):
  $$Z = \text{round}\left( -\frac{\alpha}{S} \right) + q_{min}$$

For symmetric quantization (commonly used for convolutional weights):
$$\alpha = -\beta \implies Z = 0, \quad S = \frac{\max(|x|)}{127}$$

### 3.2 Quantized Convolution Arithmetic
Consider a convolution layer computing $Y = W X + B$. With quantized inputs $q_X$, weights $q_W$, and bias $q_B$:

$$S_Y (q_Y - Z_Y) = \left( S_W q_W \right) \left( S_X (q_X - Z_X) \right) + S_B q_B$$

$$q_Y = Z_Y + \frac{S_W S_X}{S_Y} \sum \left( q_W \cdot (q_X - Z_X) \right) + \frac{S_B}{S_Y} q_B$$

The term $M = \frac{S_W S_X}{S_Y}$ is a constant scalar multiplier ($0 < M < 1$), which is decomposed into fixed-point integer arithmetic:

$$M \approx 2^{-n} M_0 \quad \text{where } M_0 \in \mathbb{Z}, n \in \mathbb{N}$$

This allows the edge processor to evaluate convolutions using solely INT32 accumulators and bit-shifts, completely bypassing floating-point hardware units.

### 3.3 Roofline Model for Edge Inference
The operational throughput of the Drishti pipeline on hardware is governed by the Roofline Model:

$$\text{Attainable Performance } [ \text{GFLOP/s} ] = \min\left( \Pi_{peak}, \; \beta_{mem} \times I \right)$$

where:
- $\Pi_{peak}$ is the theoretical peak compute capability (GFLOP/s or GOP/s).
- $\beta_{mem}$ is the sustained memory bandwidth (GB/s).
- $I$ is the arithmetic intensity:
  $$I = \frac{\text{Total Operations (FLOPs)}}{\text{Total Memory Traffic (Bytes)}}$$

| Platform | Compute Peak ($\Pi_{peak}$) | Memory Bandwidth ($\beta_{mem}$) | Balance Point ($I^* = \Pi / \beta$) | Typical YOLO Nano Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Raspberry Pi 5 (4x A76 @ 2.4GHz)** | $\sim 76.8 \text{ GFLOPS (FP32)}$ | $\sim 17.0 \text{ GB/s (LPDDR4X)}$ | $4.51 \text{ FLOP/Byte}$ | Compute Bound in INT8 |
| **Hailo-8L NPU (Pi AI Kit)** | $\sim 13 \text{ TOPS (INT8)}$ | Dedicated On-Chip SRAM Fabric | Architecture-dependent | Compute Bound |
| **PC Host (NVIDIA RTX 4050 Laptop)** | $\sim 9.0 \text{ TFLOPS (FP32)}$ | $\sim 192 \text{ GB/s (GDDR6)}$ | $46.8 \text{ FLOP/Byte}$ | Compute Bound |

By quantizing YOLO weights from FP32 (4 bytes/param) to INT8 (1 byte/param), memory bus footprint drops by $75\%$, avoiding memory-bandwidth starvation on the Raspberry Pi 5 memory bus.

## 4. Psychoacoustics & Cognitive Load Theory

### 4.1 Auditory Bandwidth Bottleneck
Human visual processing bandwidth is on the order of $\sim 10^7 \text{ bits/s}$ (parallel spatial processing across millions of photoreceptors). Conversely, human speech comprehension is strictly serial, operating at an informational throughput of only $\sim 30 - 50 \text{ bits/s}$ ($\sim 150 \text{ words/min}$).

To avoid cognitive paralysis and auditory fatigue:
1. **Urgent Safety Alerts** must bypass linguistic parsing entirely, using spatialized acoustic clicks or tones.
2. **Directional Spatial Audio** encodes 3D azimuth and proximity directly into acoustic wave geometry.
3. **Verbal Descriptions** are restricted to on-demand triggers or low-frequency semantic updates.

### 4.2 Binaural Cues: ITD and ILD
To synthesize virtual 3D sound sources through bone-conduction or stereo headphones, Drishti implements the duplex theory of spatial hearing.

#### Interaural Time Difference (ITD)
Sound originating at azimuth angle $\theta \in [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$ reaches the closer ear before the farther ear due to acoustic path length difference. Modeling the human head as a rigid sphere of radius $r \approx 0.0875\text{ m}$ (Woodworth's model):

$$\Delta t_{ITD}(\theta) = \begin{cases} \frac{r}{c} \sin\theta & \text{if sound is in the far field and } |\theta| \le \frac{\pi}{2} \\ \frac{r}{c} (\theta + \sin\theta) & \text{general Woodworth curved path} \end{cases}$$

At maximum lateral azimuth ($\theta = 90^\circ = \pi/2$ rad):
$$\Delta t_{ITD, max} \approx \frac{0.0875}{343} \left(\frac{\pi}{2} + 1\right) \approx 0.655 \text{ ms} = 655 \;\mu\text{s}$$

#### Interaural Level Difference (ILD)
For frequencies above $\sim 1.5\text{ kHz}$, the acoustic wavelength $\lambda = c/f$ is smaller than the head diameter ($2r \approx 0.175\text{ m}$):

$$\lambda_{1.5\text{kHz}} = \frac{343}{1500} \approx 0.228\text{ m}$$

The head acts as an acoustic shadow obstacle, attenuating the sound level at the farther ear:

$$\text{ILD}(\theta) = 20 \log_{10} \left( \frac{P_{ipsilateral}}{P_{contralateral}} \right) \approx \gamma \cdot \sin\theta \quad [\text{dB}]$$

where $\gamma$ is a frequency-dependent attenuation coefficient ($\gamma \approx 6 \text{ to } 20\text{ dB}$).

### 4.3 Distance Cueing via Audio Frequency & Repetition Rate
To convey proximity $d$:
- **Pulse repetition frequency (PRF)**: $f_{pulse} \propto \frac{1}{d}$ (analogous to a sonar ping rate, scaling from 1 Hz at 3 m to 10 Hz at 0.5 m).
- **Fundamental pitch modulation**: Frequency shifts from 440 Hz (far) to 880 Hz (critical threshold $< 1\text{ m}$).

## 5. Multilingual Indic OCR from First Principles

### 5.1 Text Detection via Differentiable Binarization (DBNet)
Traditional segmentation applies a rigid step function to probability maps, preventing gradient propagation during training. Differentiable Binarization replaces hard thresholding with an approximate differentiable sigmoid step:

$$\hat{B}_{i,j} = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left( -s \cdot (P_{i,j} - T_{i,j}) \right)}$$

where:
- $P_{i,j}$ is the probability map predicting whether pixel $(i,j)$ belongs to a text region.
- $T_{i,j}$ is a learned threshold map.
- $s$ is an amplification scaling factor (typically $s = 50$).

This yields precise polygon boundaries even for curved or perspective-distorted text on edge hardware.

### 5.2 Sequence Recognition via CTC Loss
Text recognition maps an image strip of height $H=32$ and width $W$ to a sequence of characters $\mathbf{l} = (l_1, \dots, l_U)$ without requiring per-character spatial segmentation.

A Convolutional-Recurrent network outputs a sequence of probability distributions $\mathbf{y} = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ over the alphabet $\Sigma \cup \{\epsilon\}$ (where $\epsilon$ is a blank token).

The conditional probability of a target label sequence $\mathbf{l}$ is the sum over all valid alignment paths $\pi$:

$$P(\mathbf{l} | \mathbf{x}) = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}(\mathbf{l})} P(\pi | \mathbf{x}) = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}(\mathbf{l})} \prod_{t=1}^T y_{\pi_t}^t$$

where $\mathcal{B}$ is the collapse operator that removes adjacent duplicates and blanks (e.g., $\mathcal{B}(\text{a } \epsilon \text{ a a}) = \text{a a}$).

The objective is minimizing the negative log-likelihood:
$$\mathcal{L}_{CTC} = -\ln P(\mathbf{l} | \mathbf{x})$$

### 5.3 Indic Script Morphology & Complexities
Unlike Latin scripts which are strictly linear, Indic scripts (Devanagari for Hindi, Malayalam, and Tamil) are alphasyllabaries (Abugidas):
1. **Consonant-Vowel Ligatures**: A consonant character (e.g., Devanagari क) combines with a dependent vowel sign (Matra, e.g., ि) to form a single visual grapheme cluster (कि), which may visually appear *before* the consonant despite being pronounced *after*.
2. **Conjunct Consonants (Samyuktaksharas)**: Two or more consonants without intervening vowels merge into a distinct composite ligature (e.g., क् + ष = क्ष).
3. **Unicode Canonical Normalization**: Text must be preprocessed using Unicode Normalization Form C (NFC) to ensure consistent token representations before TTS speech generation.

## 6. Vision-Language Modeling (VLM) for Scene Narration

### 6.1 Architecture Overview (SmolVLM / Moondream2)
The VLM consists of three decoupled components:
1. **Vision Transformer (ViT) Backbone**: Decomposes an input image $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ into non-overlapping patches $\mathbf{x}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (P^2 C)}$ ($P=14$ or $16$). Patches are linearly projected into $D$-dimensional embeddings, prepended with position encodings, and processed through self-attention layers to produce visual token sequence $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D_v}$.
2. **Cross-Modal Projector**: A lightweight 2-layer MLP or cross-attention resampler projecting visual dimension $D_v \to D_{text}$.
3. **Autoregressive Language Decoder**: A decoder-only transformer (e.g., 500M parameter SmolLM2) that attends jointly over prompt tokens and visual tokens.

### 6.2 Prompt Conditioning for Visual Impairment
General VLMs generate verbose, passive descriptions ("An image featuring a room with..."). Drishti conditions the model using structured system prefixes designed for navigational utility:
```
System: You are Drishti, an assistive navigator for a visually impaired user.
Rules:
1. State immediate obstacles and distance first.
2. Specify directional orientation (left, right, straight ahead).
3. Mention key navigational hazards (drop-offs, low overheads, steps).
4. Limit response to 2 crisp, actionable sentences.
```

## 7. Edge Privacy & Caregiver Telemetry

### 7.1 Local Face Anonymization
Before any snapshot is buffered or transmitted via the caregiver channel, faces are detected and redacted locally on the edge device. The redaction applies a Gaussian kernel filter $G_\sigma$:

$$I_{blurred}(x, y) = \sum_{i=-k}^k \sum_{j=-k}^k I(x+i, y+j) \frac{1}{2\pi \sigma^2} \exp\left(-\frac{i^2 + j^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$

with kernel radius $k = 15$ and variance $\sigma = 7$, rendering individual identities completely unrecoverable while preserving context (head position, posture).

### 7.2 MQTT Telemetry Protocol
Telemetry payloads are transmitted over Mosquitto MQTT using TLS 1.3 encryption (TCP port 8883) with QoS level 1 (At least once delivery with `PUBACK`).

- **Heartbeat Topic**: `drishti/device/{device_id}/heartbeat`
- **Telemetry Payload Schema**:
```json
{
  "timestamp": 1776150000,
  "battery_pct": 84,
  "cpu_temp_c": 52.4,
  "system_state": "ACTIVE_NAVIGATION",
  "last_distance_m": 1.42,
  "gps": {
    "lat": 12.9716,
    "lon": 77.5946,
    "accuracy_m": 3.2
  }
}
```
- **Last Will and Testament (LWT)**: The broker automatically broadcasts a `STATUS_OFFLINE` payload to `drishti/device/{device_id}/status` if the TCP socket disconnects unexpectedly.
